## Most decision-relevant corrections

1. **Telegram Ads cannot use the gate or the redirect domain** (rows 20-21). Ads guidelines §4.3 ban "mostly
   noninteractive bots designed to redirect elsewhere", mock destinations for other ads, and auto-redirecting websites.
   Telegram Ads traffic must go straight to the main bot with a start parameter. So design A covers only
   TikTok, blogger and own-post traffic.
2. **§10's affiliation clause names channels and communities, not bots** (rows 2-3, 10). The main bot is exposed
   because "you" is the developer and Telegram decides at its discretion, not because the bot is "affiliated". Our own
   announcement channel and community chats are what an ad-bot ban could take along. The ad bot must link only to the
   main bot. "Lowers the risk, cannot remove it" still holds.
3. **The exchange receives our users' Telegram IDs as well as the token** (row 14). Telega.io asks the owner to upload and
   refresh a user-ID database. That is a third-party transfer of 14-17-year-olds' IDs (152-FZ), and it is missing from 14.
4. **The ≈ 20 % break-even is a ceiling** (rows 23, 26, 28). It compares A with no ad bot (range 11-45 %).
   - Against design B it is lower by the factor (1 - opt-in share).
   - An ads-only bot gives users every reason to block it, so 11's main-bot reachable base is an upper bound for A.
5. **The FAS no-liability window ends 2026-12-31** (row 30). A growth-phase launch would most likely run after it. Under R3
   this is a fact for the timeline, not a blocker.
6. **The no-respawn rule and ownership transfer have better support than 14 claims** (rows 8, 15).
   - §5.2(f) itself says "circumvent bans".
   - BotFather transfer is DOCUMENTED in core.telegram.org/bots/features (only to a user who has interacted with the
     bot; permanent).
   - Create the ad bot directly on the second account, not by transfer or through Managed Bots.
